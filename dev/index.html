Home

TensorMixedStates (TMS) is a Julia library to make simulations of closed or open quantum systems using Matrix Product States representations.

Version 2.0 is a rewrite of the library: a program written for 1.x needs the edits listed in Upgrading from 1.x.

Features

  • Pure states and, for open systems, density matrices, both represented as matrix product states.
  • A rich set of sites and operators, easily extended by the user, with a very expressive syntax for observables, gates, channels, Hamiltonians and Lindbladians.
  • Ground states and excited states with DMRG, Hamiltonian and Lindbladian evolution with TDVP and with the WI and WII approximations, steady states, and thermal states by evolution in imaginary time.
  • Prepared states: GHZ, Dicke and W states, graph states, singlets on pairs of sites, the AKLT state, Slater determinants and Fermi seas of free fermions, and the fully mixed states of a sector.
  • Gates and channels, noisy gates included, and the measurement of a state with its probabilities, its samples and its collapse.
  • Measurements of expectation values, correlations, entanglement entropies, purities, fidelities and reduced density matrices.
  • Conserved quantities, with weak and strong symmetries for open systems.
  • An optional high level interface, which writes a simulation in a few lines, with its measurements, its output files and checkpoints, from which an interrupted simulation resumes.

Being based on ITensor, TMS delivers high performance computations, and uses the cores of the machine, see Threads and performance.

Installation

To use TMS, you need to have Julia installed on your system. Installing Julia is usually easy and fast, see The Julia Programming Language for instructions. TMS requires at least Julia version 1.10.5 to run.

To install TMS in Julia, launch the Julia interface (by typing julia on the command line) and type

]add TensorMixedStates

The ] switches the prompt to the package manager of Julia.

After downloading TMS, Julia compiles it, with no interaction on the user's part, the first time and after each update of TMS or of a package it uses. This compilation runs small computations, so that the first calls of a script do not wait for their own compilation, and takes five to six minutes and up to 7 GB of memory, 9 GB with Julia 1.10. On a machine with less memory, a login node of a cluster for instance, these computations are left out by a file LocalPreferences.toml next to the Project.toml of the environment, written before installing TMS or before the next using TensorMixedStates:

[TensorMixedStates]
precompile_workload = false

The compilation of TMS then takes about ten seconds and less than 1 GB, the packages it uses taking about 2 GB the first time, but each new session compiles what it uses as it runs: the first simulation of High Level Interface takes about three minutes rather than six seconds.

A first example

The ground state of a transverse field Ising chain of ten qubits, and a few measurements on it:

using TensorMixedStates, .Qubits

n = 10
mysystem = System(n, Qubit(conserve = parity(N)))
hamiltonian = -sum(X(i)X(i + 1) for i in 1:n-1) - 0.5 * sum(Z(i) for i in 1:n)
energy, ground = ground_state(hamiltonian, State{Pure}(mysystem, "Up");
                              algo = Dmrg(nsweeps = 10), limits = Limits(maxdim = 20))
measure(ground, [X(1)X(n), Z])

The hamiltonian flips the spins two by two, so it conserves the parity of the number of down spins, and the qubits are told so: the state stays in the sector it starts in, that of the ground state. energy is about -9.7655, and measure gives the correlation between the two ends of the chain, about 0.748, and the magnetization along z on every site. The Manual explains each of these steps.

Using TMS

To use TMS in your code you need to write

using TensorMixedStates

Julia script names are usually written with a .jl extension. Once you have written your script, you can execute it with

julia --threads=auto my_script.jl

or julia -t auto my_script.jl for short, which starts Julia with as many threads as the machine has; julia my_script.jl runs the script too, on a single thread of Julia. What the threads bring, and how TMS uses them, is in Threads and performance.

You can also use TMS in the interactive Julia interpreter.

Learning about matrix product states

For an introduction to matrix product states and tensor networks, see the lecture notes of the course Grégoire Misguich gave at the 9th Les Houches summer school on Computational Physics: Open Quantum Systems, in June 2026, available on arXiv:2606.24803. Their repository holds the sixteen Julia examples the notes use, some of them written with TMS.

Examples

Working examples are in the folder examples of the repository, in three folders.

The folder low_level holds computations written directly, each checking its result against an exact value:

  • spin_one_chain.jl: the ground state and the first excitation of the spin one Heisenberg chain, as an excited state and as the ground state of a sector;
  • free_fermions.jl: the Fermi sea of a hopping chain and the correlations of its ground state, conserving the number of fermions;
  • domain_wall_quench.jl: a domain wall melting in an XX chain, with Tdvp, ApproxW and half the time step;
  • driven_damped_qubits.jl: qubits driven and jumping from up to down, relaxing to their steady state, found also by steady_state;
  • thermal_state.jl: the thermal state of an Ising chain, its energy and its partition function;
  • noisy_ghz.jl: a GHZ state made by a circuit and depolarized, its fidelity, the statistics of its parity, and a post-selection;
  • entanglement.jl: entropies, reduced density matrix, negativity and mutual information on GHZ and Werner states;
  • strong_then_weak.jl: fermions dephasing under a strong symmetry, then losing particles under a weak one.

The folder high_level holds simulations written with the high level interface:

  • dmrg.jl: a ground state search with GroundState, stopped by its tolerance;
  • excited_states.jl: the ground state and the first excited state of the frustrated chain at its Majumdar-Ghosh point, through a named state;
  • ising_quench.jl: a quench of an Ising chain with periodic boundary conditions, evolved with ApproxW;
  • precession.jl: qubits precessing in a field, checked against the exact solution with a Check;
  • gates.jl: gates applied to a state with the Gates phase;
  • measured_trajectory.jl: a chain of qubits kicked and measured at each step, a quantum trajectory written with an Update phase;
  • thermal_phase.jl: the thermal states of an XXZ chain over a range of temperatures, written as JSON Lines;
  • two_time_correlations.jl: the correlations at two times of an Ising chain, two states evolved together by one Evolve and measured by Overlap;
  • complete_graph_tdvp.jl: a complete graph state decaying under dissipation, evolved with Tdvp;
  • fermion_chain_conserved.jl: a fermion chain with dephasing conserving its number of particles strongly, then weakly once a loss is added.

The folder article holds the six examples of the reference article:

  • 1_Fermion_chain_with_dephasing.jl: a spinless fermion chain with dephasing, from an alternating state;
  • 2_XX_spin_chain.jl: an XX spin chain with dissipation at its two ends, from the infinite temperature state;
  • 3_Free_bosons_with_source.jl and 4_Free_fermions_with_source.jl: free bosons, or free fermions, injected at the centre of an empty chain;
  • 5_Complete_graph_decoherence.jl: the graph state of a complete graph decaying under dissipation;
  • 6_Brickwall.jl: a brickwall circuit of Rxx and Rzz gates with depolarizing noise.

Module

TensorMixedStates — Module
TensorMixedStates

a library for simulating closed and open quantum systems with matrix product states. A state is either pure, its wave function as an MPS, or mixed, its density matrix as an MPS, on which Lindbladian evolution and noisy gates act.

using TensorMixedStates, .Qubits
sys = System(10, Qubit())
ham = -sum(Z(i) * Z(i + 1) for i in 1:9) - 0.5 * sum(X(i) for i in 1:10)
E, ψ = ground_state(ham, random_state(Pure, sys, 8); algo = Dmrg(nsweeps = 10))
ρ = evolve(-im * ham + sum(Dissipator(sqrt(0.1) * Sm)(i) for i in 1:10), 1.0, mix(ψ);
           nsteps = 20, limits = Limits(maxdim = 64))
measure(ρ, [Z, X(1) * X(10), Purity])

The evolver is -im * ham plus dissipators, "Up" is $|0 angle$ on a qubit, and a value is real for a Hermitian operator. The page "Quick reference" of the documentation, shipped with the package as joinpath(pkgdir(TensorMixedStates), "docs", "src", "quickref.md"), gives the whole library on one page, and "Upgrading from 1.x" what changed since version 1.
