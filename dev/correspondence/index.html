Coming from QuTiP or ITensors

From QuTiP

QuTiP holds states and operators as dense matrices, TMS as matrix product states and operators, which reach much larger systems at the price of a truncation, see Checking the accuracy. The conventions are the same: on a qubit, QuTiP's basis(2, 0) is "Up", with $\sigma_z = +1$, sigmap() is Sp and sigmam() is Sm, and a collapse operator c of mesolve is Dissipator(c) in an evolver.

QuTiPTMS
basis(2, 0), basis(2, 1) on each of n qubitsState{Pure}(System(n, Qubit()), "Up"), "Dn"
tensor(basis(2, 0), basis(2, 1))State{Pure}(System(2, Qubit()), ["Up", "Dn"])
ket2dm(ψ)mix(ψ)
sigmax(), sigmay(), sigmaz() on qubit iX(i), Y(i), Z(i)
sigmap(), sigmam()Sp, Sm
destroy(d) on boson iA(i) on Boson(d)
qeye(d)Id
tensor(sigmaz(), sigmaz()) acting on qubits i and jZ(i) * Z(j)
mesolve(H, ρ0, tlist, c_ops = [c1, c2], e_ops = [o])evolve(-im * H + Dissipator(c1) + Dissipator(c2), t, ρ0; nsteps), then measure(ρ, o)
sesolve(H, ψ0, tlist)evolve(-im * H, t, ψ0; nsteps)
steadystate(H, c_ops)steady_state(-im * H + sum(Dissipator.(c_ops)), ρ; algo = Dmrg(; nsweeps))
H.groundstate()ground_state(H, ψ; algo = Dmrg(; nsweeps))
expect(o, ρ)expect(ρ, o), or measure(ρ, o)
ρ.ptrace([0, 2])partial_trace(ρ, [1, 3]; keep = true), sites counted from 1
entropy_vn(ρ.ptrace([0]))vonneumann_entropy(ρ, [1])

A collapse operator carries its rate, sqrt(γ) * c in QuTiP as in TMS: Dissipator(sqrt(γ) * c) is γ * Dissipator(c). A QuTiP operator is a matrix on the whole space; a TMS operator is placed on sites, Z(3), and an operator of one site given alone, Z, is measured on every site. Where mesolve returns the values at each time of tlist, a TMS program evolves step by step and measures between the steps, or measures in an Evolve phase, see High Level Interface.

From ITensors

TMS is built on ITensors and ITensorMPS: a pure state holds an MPS, state.state, on the indices siteinds(Pure, system), and its algorithms call those of ITensorMPS. What it adds is the density matrix, held as an MPS too, the operators written as expressions rather than OpSum, the Lindbladians, the channels, the measurements and the simulations.

A state caches what it computes from its MPS: a function of ITensorMPS changing an MPS in place, as orthogonalize! or truncate!, must not be applied to state.state, and State(state, mps) makes the state of a changed copy.

ITensors and ITensorMPSTMS
s = siteinds("Qubit", n)System(n, Qubit())
MPS(s, "0")State{Pure}(System(n, Qubit()), "Up")
random_mps(s; linkdims = 8)random_state(Pure, system, 8)
os = OpSum(); os += "Z", 1, "Z", 2; MPO(os, s)Z(1) * Z(2), made an MPO when it is used, or make_mpo(state, op)
dmrg(H, ψ0; nsweeps, maxdim, cutoff)ground_state(H, ψ0; algo = Dmrg(; nsweeps), limits = Limits(; maxdim, cutoff))
tdvp(H, -im * t, ψ0; nsteps)evolve(-im * H, t, ψ0; nsteps)
expect(ψ, "Z"), correlation_matrix(ψ, "Z", "Z")expect1(ψ, Z), expect2(ψ, (Z, Z)), or measure(ψ, [Z, (Z, Z)])
apply(gates, ψ; cutoff)apply(gates, ψ; limits), the gates written as operators, CX(1, 2) * H(1) of Circuits
op("Rxx", s1, s2; ϕ), $e^{-i\phi X \otimes X}$Rxx(2ϕ)(i, j) of Circuits, $e^{-i\theta X \otimes X/2}$ as in Qiskit
inner(ψ, ϕ)inner(ψ, ϕ)

tdvp of ITensorMPS computes $e^{t A}$ for its operator $A$ and time $t$, so that a real time evolution under $H$ takes -im * t; evolve takes the evolver -im * H and the real time t, so that the same -im is written on the operator. A Lindbladian is an evolver as well, on a mixed state, which ITensorMPS has no notion of.
